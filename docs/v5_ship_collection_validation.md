## Selection Logic

1. Start from MLIT, not memory.
   MLIT lists scheduled routes that have official web sources, including
   municipal and island routes. This is the national discovery baseline.
2. Classify, do not immediately promote.
   Each MLIT entry becomes one of:
   - `scheduled_public_candidate`
   - `municipal_scheduled_candidate`
   - `review_transport_or_sightseeing`
3. Prioritize by gameplay value.
   Long-distance/night ferries, airport sea links, large regional shortcuts,
   and island trunk routes are first collection waves.
4. Keep local routes alive.
   Small island ferries and municipal渡船 stay in discovery instead of being
   discarded; they become playable when connectors and official fares/timetables
   are attached.
5. Exclude only after review.
   Sightseeing/water-bus routes are not auto-playable, but are kept as review
   candidates because some water buses function as real point-to-point transport.
6. Never fake missing fields.
   No playable promotion without official timetable, calendar, fare, port
   coordinates, and rail/bus/walk connectors.

## Reliability Rules Before Collection

- Treat `docs/v5_ship_collection_backlog.md` as a prioritized collection wave,
  not as a claim that every Japanese local ferry is already enumerated in detail.
- Treat the MLIT operator page as the discovery baseline for local routes.
- Do not promote sightseeing-only routes unless they are normal scheduled
  point-to-point transport.
- Do not promote any route with unknown adult fare.
- Do not invent access edges to ports. Use rail, bus, or walking connectors
  only when the corresponding node and timing are real.
- For parallel operators on the same corridor, keep operator identity and
  de-duplicate only by exact sailing identity.
